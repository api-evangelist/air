# AIR

> AIR is the context firewall for AI agents. AIR protects AI agents by continuously analyzing and filtering the inputs entering an agent's context, including AI add-ons, websites, internal data, and other external resources.

AIR helps enterprises secure AI agents across discovery, governance, supply-chain vetting, and runtime protection.

Official website: https://www.air.security/

## Core Product

- [AIR — The Context Firewall for AI Agents](https://www.air.security/): AIR sits between agents and the outside world, analyzing and filtering inputs before they reach an agent's context.
- AIR Control: Governs the agent fleet, including sanctioned and shadow agents, identities, permissions, configurations, and policy.
- AIR Filter: The add-on firewall for AI agents. Skills, plugins, MCP servers, sub-agents, and other AI add-ons are vetted before installation and continuously over time.
- AIR Defend: Secures the actions AI agents take at runtime, with visibility, detection, response, and prevention.
- AIR Marketplace: A trusted source of pre-vetted external and certified internal AI add-ons.

## Security Research

AIR publishes original security research on AI agents, AI add-ons, skills, dependencies, marketplaces, and agent supply-chain risks.

### The Story of Skills

- [The Story of Skills — How We Hijacked 26,000 Agents With One Instagram Ad](https://www.air.security/blog-posts/the-story-of-skills)
- Published June 22, 2026.
- AIR built a malicious AI skill, promoted it publicly, and demonstrated how a skill could gain control of more than 26,000 agents while avoiding detection by public security scanners.
- The research demonstrates the risks created by unvetted AI skills, external instructions, and open marketplaces.

### The Circus of Skills

- [The Circus of Skills — 17,822 Skills and 6.7M Installations Exposed to Untrusted External Instructions](https://www.air.security/blog-posts/the-circus-of-skills)
- Published June 24, 2026.
- AIR analyzed a large corpus of AI skills and found widespread exposure to untrusted external instruction sources.
- The research highlights why security teams must evaluate the external resources that AI skills and add-ons depend on, rather than only scanning their static content.

### SkillJacking

- [SkillJacking — 925 Skills Hijacked From Their Maintainers, Affecting 134K Agents](https://www.air.security/blog-posts/skilljacking)
- Published July 2, 2026.
- AIR discovered 925 skills built on dependencies that could be hijacked, affecting more than 134,000 agents.
- The research shows that a trusted or previously benign AI skill can become dangerous when an underlying account, repository, package, domain, or other dependency changes ownership or becomes compromised.
- AIR demonstrated a proof of concept in a controlled lab environment; no malicious content was pushed to the real public skill.

### Fake It 'Till You Make It

- [Fake It 'Till You Make It — A Skill in the Wild Impersonated Anthropic to Run Arbitrary Code](https://www.air.security/blog-posts/fake-it)
- Published July 16, 2026.
- AIR found skills using trusted AI vendor names and nonexistent dependencies in ways that could mislead scanners and users.
- The research demonstrates why security systems must verify dependencies and ownership rather than trusting brand names or repository paths.

### RepoJacking

- [RepoJacking — 178 Skills Hijacked Through the Repositories They Depend On, 23,812 Agents Affected](https://www.air.security/blog-posts/repojacking)
- Published July 30, 2026.
- AIR identified 178 skills affected by hijackable repository dependencies, exposing 23,812 agents.
- The research shows how an AI skill can remain unchanged while a repository it depends on is taken over, turning an existing installation into a supply-chain attack path.
- AIR recommends continuous dependency vetting rather than one-time scanning.
